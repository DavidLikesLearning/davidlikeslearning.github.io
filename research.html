My current PhD work is on energy system optimization models, the tools planners use to figure out how a city, region, or country should build and run its power grid over the next few decades to hit cost, environmental or reliability targets.

Temoa

An open-source model that simulates how an energy system could evolve over decades, used to compare long-term investment and policy choices. I'm a collaborator on the project; a publication is in progress.

Python

GNN/GAT & Gradient Boosting for Transmission Expansion & Line Switching

Graph neural networks, graph attention networks, and gradient boosting models that learn to solve two classic grid-planning problems: where to build new transmission lines, and which existing lines to switch off to relieve congestion, checked against full AC power flow instead of the usual simplified approximation. The expansion models are path-aware, so they judge the feasibility of a line corridor. Tested on the synthetic Texas ACTIVSg2000 grid.

PythonPyTorch

InterdictionRobustOPF

Testing whether today's fastest AC optimal power flow algorithms can react quickly enough to grid damage from extreme weather or deliberate attacks, where operators need a safe, feasible response in seconds rather than minutes.

Before my PhD, I worked on making solar panel data more useful: panels generate messy, unlabeled power readings, and small problems like shade from a nearby tree, clouds, accumulated dust, or circuitry problems can cut how much energy they produce. Each of these losses has different impacts, so we can diagnose them from just looking at the power generated by the panel. This work builds tools to catch and quantify those losses automatically.

the deeper dive

A single panel's power output over a day already tells you a lot. A clean signal traces a smooth arc as the sun rises and sets. Soiling (dust, pollen, grime) quietly shaves that whole arc down. A passing cloud instead carves a sharp dip at one moment, then recovers. Separating those two signatures from raw power data, without needing a label telling you which is which, is the core problem this line of work solves.

Modeled from a clear-sky irradiance curve (pvlib, Austin TX, Jun 21), not a measured panel, with the cloud, soiling, and obstruction effects layered on top to show the signal shapes this work learns to tell apart.

I've also worked on applying machine learning to healthcare problems, both building models for clinical data and helping design programs that bring more students into healthcare research. I wrote about how the Stanford Data Ocean helped thousands of people access quality healthcare and biostatistics education for free here.

2022

Development and validation of a machine learning system to identify reflux events

Trained a machine learning model to automatically flag acid reflux events in 24-hour esophageal monitoring data, cutting down hours of manual review by doctors. We tried deep neural nets, LSTMs, and even some Fourier analysis methods. Transformers resulted the best overall.
