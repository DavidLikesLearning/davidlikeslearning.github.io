research, in plain english

What I actually work on, explained without the jargon — full publication list on Google Scholar

My current PhD work is on energy system optimization models — the tools planners use to figure out how a city, region, or country should build and run its power grid over the next few decades to hit cost and emissions targets.

Temoa

An open-source model that simulates how an energy system could evolve over decades, used to compare long-term investment and policy choices. I'm a collaborator on the project; a publication is in progress.

Python

Before my PhD, I worked on making solar panel data more useful: panels generate messy, unlabeled power readings, and small problems like shade from a nearby tree can quietly cut into how much energy they produce. This work builds tools to catch and quantify that automatically.

2022

Solar panel power simulation for shade detection

Built a simulator that predicts how much power a shaded solar panel loses, so utilities can spot shading problems without needing labeled data.

Estimation of shade losses in unlabeled PV data

Developed a method to estimate how much energy solar panels lose to shade using only their raw power output, no extra sensors or labels required.
