research

Selected publications: plain-English deep dive · full list on Google Scholar

My current PhD work is on energy system optimization models, the tools planners use to figure out how a city, region, or country should build and run its power grid over the next few decades to hit cost, environmental or reliability targets.

Temoa

An open-source model that simulates how an energy system could evolve over decades, used to compare long-term investment and policy choices. I'm a collaborator on the project; a publication is in progress.

PythonGurobi

GNN/GAT & Gradient Boosting for Transmission Expansion & Line Switching

Testing ML methods to find where to build new transmission lines, and which existing lines to switch off to relieve congestion, checked against full AC power flow. The expansion models are path-aware, so they judge the feasibility of a line corridor on land use, geographic, and environmental factors from OpenStreetMap Tested on the synthetic Texas ACTIVSg2000 grid.

Before my PhD, I worked on making solar panel data more useful: panels generate messy, unlabeled power readings, and small problems like shade from a nearby tree, clouds, accumulated dust, or circuitry problems can cut how much energy they produce. Each of these losses has different impacts, so we can diagnose them from just looking at the power generated by the panel. This work builds tools to catch and quantify those losses automatically.

GNN/GAT & Gradient Boosting for Transmission Expansion & Line Switching

Graph neural networks, graph attention networks, and gradient boosting models that learn to solve transmission expansion and optimal line switching against full AC power flow. The expansion models are path-aware, so they judge whether an entire line corridor is buildable, not just its endpoints. Tested on the synthetic Texas ACTIVSg2000 grid.

fun

A few things I love doing around Pittsburgh (and a bit beyond) : )

Libby & The StoryGraph

Free ebooks and audiobooks through your local library card via Libby, and sharing recommendations with friends on The StoryGraph, another wonderful free app.

Ohiopyle whitewater rafting

Rapids on the Youghiogheny River at Ohiopyle State Park, about an hour southeast of Pittsburgh: some of the best whitewater on the East Coast.

POGOH bike share

Pittsburgh's nonprofit bike share. If you're lucky there are electric bikes to battle the three river's hills. And the PRT buses have two bike racks per bus so it's kind of a magical place for car-free transportation between the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers.

Great Allegheny Passage (GAP Trail)

A 150+ mile rail trail running from Pittsburgh toward Washington, D.C. I've never explored most of its miles but I'm a fan of the ones near CMU and Schenley Park.

New River Gorge National Park

Camping, climbing, and rafting in West Virginia's newest national park. Here my friends and I learned that DRY wood helps a lot to start fires. And toilet paper is a fantastic starter material. I assume it's the porosity.

Phipps Conservatory and Botanical Gardens

A beautiful glasshouse and botanical garden right in Schenley Park. Worth a visit most weeks, and partciularly wonderful when the outside is a crisp -3 degrees F.

Schenley Park & Frick Park

Two of Pittsburgh's biggest and most woodsy city parks, maintained by the Pittsburgh Parks Conservancy: my regular jogging and call-grandma-while-I-walk spots.

Pittsburgh farmers markets

CitiParks runs farmers markets across the city all season. They're just great. A cheaper lunch than some restaurants, and with the added joy of local community.
